Part A · Computer vision

Digital image processing, step by step

PHASE 1 · ACQUISITION

Field capture and safety gate 1

Lab photos are clean, but field photos are not. The capture model multiplies the leaf by a smooth illumination field (a random-direction gradient blended with an off-centre vignette) and adds Gaussian sensor noise. Keeping the noise-free version gives a reference, so restoration can be measured objectively later.

reference = G(x,y) · f(x,y) captured = reference + N(0, σ²)

Gate 1 rejects photos whose mean grey level is outside [35, 235]: too dark or blown out to trust.

PHASE 2 · RESTORATION

Restoration is objective

Restoration models the degradation and inverts it, so success has a number. Here the noise model is known, and the result is scored against the noise-free reference with PSNR and SSIM.

Non-local means averages pixels whose surrounding 7×7 patches look alike within a 21×21 window. Median and Gaussian filters average only over a fixed neighbourhood, so they blur lesion edges more.

NLM: û(p) = Σ_q w(p,q) u(q) / Σ_q w(p,q) w(p,q) = exp(−‖P(p) − P(q)‖² / h²)
PHASE 3 · ENHANCEMENT

Enhancement is subjective

Enhancement makes the image easier to see or analyse. There is no ground truth, so it is judged by its effect: contrast, entropy and how the histogram spreads.

CLAHE equalises the histogram tile by tile (8×8 tiles), clips each tile's histogram at 2× the mean to stop noise blowing up, and blends neighbouring tiles bilinearly. It runs on the L channel of Lab only, so lesion colour, a key disease cue, is untouched.

PHASE 4 · COMPRESSION

Transmission over a thin uplink

A raw 128×128 RGB image is 49,152 bytes. JPEG converts to YCbCr, subsamples colour, takes an 8×8 DCT, and quantises high frequencies away; lower quality means coarser quantisation. The receiver, and therefore the CNN, only ever sees the decoded image.

The error map shows where information was lost: 8×8 block edges and fine texture such as lesion borders.

ratio = (H · W · 3) / bytes sent
PHASE 5 · MORPHOLOGY AND SEGMENTATION

Find the leaf, discard the background

Leaves are saturated green; paper and soil are grey. Otsu's method picks the saturation threshold that maximises between-class variance.

Morphology then cleans the binary image: opening (erode, then dilate, 5×5 ellipse) removes speckle, closing (dilate, then erode, 7×7 ellipse ×2) bridges gaps. The largest connected component is kept, and hole filling restores lesions that thresholded as background. The leaf is masked, cropped to its bounding box and resized for the CNN.

Gate 2 rejects masks covering less than 5% or more than 95% of the frame, because segmentation has failed in those cases.

Part B · Neural network basics

Recognition with a convolutional network

PHASE 6 · RECOGNITION

What the CNN sees

A convolution slides small learned 3×3 filters over the image; each output channel is a feature map showing where its pattern occurs. ReLU keeps positive responses. Max pooling halves the resolution, so deeper layers see larger regions. Early maps respond to edges and colour; later ones to lesion-like texture.

Global average pooling turns 128 maps into 128 numbers, and a linear layer gives two logits. Softmax with temperature T (fitted on validation data) turns them into a calibrated P(diseased), which the agent needs.

P(diseased) = e^(z₁/T) / (e^(z₀/T) + e^(z₁/T))
Part C · Agent types deep dive

A utility-based agent decides under uncertainty

PHASE 7 · DECISION

Minimise expected cost, not error count

A simple reflex agent would threshold at 0.5. A utility-based agent weighs outcomes: missing a diseased leaf costs far more than an unnecessary inspection, so it flags at a much lower probability.

E[CLEAR] = p · C_FN E[FLAG] = (1 − p) · C_FP FLAG when p > C_FP / (C_FP + C_FN)

RE-IMAGE buys information: when p falls in an uncertain band, or a safety gate fails, paying C_RI for a second photo is cheaper on average than guessing. The band [t_low, t_high] is chosen on the validation set. On the retake, a failed gate never clears.
